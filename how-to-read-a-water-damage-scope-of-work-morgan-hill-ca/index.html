How to Read a Water Damage Scope of Work in Morgan Hill, CA

The scope is the contract in plain language. Read it before you sign, and you avoid most disputes.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

A water damage scope of work lists the rooms affected, the water category, the tasks the crew will do, the equipment, the monitoring plan and what is excluded. Read it for three things: what is included, what is not and how changes are approved. If anything is vague, ask for a written clarification before the work starts.

The scope is different from the estimate. The estimate puts prices on the work. The scope describes the work in words. Both should match.

The parts of a scope

A well-written scope has recognizable sections.

Scope sections and what to look for.
SectionWhat to look forQuestion to ask
Project descriptionAddress, date, cause of lossIs the cause stated correctly?
Water category and classCategory 1, 2 or 3, and evaporation classHow was it judged?
Affected areasRooms, floors, square footageDoes it match what you saw?
TasksExtraction, demolition, drying, cleaningWhich are included?
Equipment and monitoringUnit counts, daily readingsHow will drying be proven?
ExclusionsRebuild, permits, contentsWhat is left for someone else?

Water category and class

ANSI/IICRC S500 sorts water into three categories by contamination. It also sorts losses into classes by how much surface area is wet and how absorbent the materials are. These two labels set the plan.

If the scope says Category 1 but the source was a washer overflow or a creek, ask why. The category affects what is thrown away. Our water extraction page includes a table of the three categories.

Task wording to watch

Each phrase is fine on its own. The point is whether it connects to a measurement.

  • Remove wet drywall: how high, and is it measured from readings?
  • Dry in place: which materials, and what is the fallback if they do not dry?
  • Antimicrobial application: where, and why?
  • Controlled demolition: what gets bagged, and who disposes of it?
  • Monitor until dry: how many visits and who signs off?

Exclusions and add-ons

Scopes often exclude rebuild work, permits, plumbing repair and contents cleaning. That is normal, but you should know who covers each. Ask if hidden damage found behind walls will be priced as an add-on and how you approve it.

If mold turns up, the scope may change. Our mold remediation page describes how containment and removal are added to a plan.

Daily logs and sign-off

Drying should end with documented moisture readings. Ask for the logs and for the final readings against the dry standard for each material. Sign off only when you have seen them.

Keep the logs with your claim file. The California Department of Insurance advises homeowners to keep records and receipts through a claim.

A quick reading drill

Read the scope once for sense and once with a highlighter. Mark every task you do not understand, every number that is not explained and every phrase such as as needed or if applicable. Those are the places to ask questions.

Then compare the scope to the photos and moisture map. The rooms and sizes should line up. If a room appears in the photos but not the scope, ask why.

Finally, compare the scope to the estimate. Each task should map to a price line. A task without a price or a price without a task is a gap to close.

Change orders and approvals

A change order is a written update to the scope. It should describe the new work, the reason and the price. Do not give verbal approval without a document, even on a busy day.

Keep each change order with the original scope. If the insurer is paying, send copies right away so the adjuster can review.

A clear change process protects both sides. It also shows how the company manages surprises, which is useful to know before work begins.

Terms you may see

Scopes use trade words that can confuse. Air mover means a high-velocity fan. LGR dehumidifier means a low grain refrigerant unit that dries air efficiently. Containment means plastic barriers that isolate a work area.

Flood cut means cutting drywall a set height above the floor to expose wet material. Antimicrobial means a treatment applied to limit microbial growth. Pack-out means moving contents out of the work area.

If any word is unclear, ask the company to define it. A good one will gladly explain.

Photos and documentation to expect

A scope should come with, or promise, before photos, daily readings, equipment placement notes and final readings. These records protect you and the crew.

Ask how you will receive them, such as by email or a shared folder. Save them with your claim file and keep a copy.

What this means in Morgan Hill

Check whether the scope says a permit is needed. City permit rules cover building repairs and alterations, while painting and carpeting are exempt, so the scope should state which side each line falls on.

If you are in San Jose, the Permit Center at 200 E. Santa Clara Street decides. For San Martin or other unincorporated places, the county does. See South San Jose and Coyote for local context.
