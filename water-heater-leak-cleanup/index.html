Emergency Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Morgan Hill, CA

A failed tank can release dozens of gallons at once. Shut off the supply, kill the power or gas to the unit and call our 24/7 line.

Call (844) 950-6900All services

A water heater leak in Morgan Hill is an emergency when water pools around the base or runs from a seam. A tank holds 40 to 80 gallons in most homes, and a failed tank can release it fast. Our dispatch line answers 24/7, so call at any hour once the unit is shut down.

Turn off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater. For an electric unit, switch off its breaker if the panel is dry. For a gas unit, set the control to pilot or off. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Our crews remove the water and dry the structure. A plumber replaces or repairs the heater, and the two visits are best scheduled close together.

Water heater emergency: shut it down safely

Do these in order, and stop if anything feels unsafe.

  1. Close the cold supply valve. It is the pipe feeding the top of the tank, usually with a lever or round handle.
  2. Cut the power source. Flip the breaker for electric units. Turn gas units to off or pilot.
  3. Do not touch wet electrical parts. If the floor is wet around the panel, call the utility.
  4. Open a hot tap upstairs. This lets air in so the tank drains and stops pushing water out.
  5. Call dispatch. Tell us where the heater sits and which rooms are wet.

Why tanks fail

Tank heaters wear out from the inside. Sediment settles at the bottom, the anode rod is consumed, and the steel lining eventually corrodes through. Morgan Hill's water is hard, with a 2021 reported hardness of about 302 mg/L, and hard water leaves more scale in a tank than soft water does.

A flush of the tank and a check of the anode rod can slow that wear. A leak from a fitting is different from a leak from the tank body. A fitting can often be repaired, while a leaking tank body means the heater is done.

Where the water goes from a garage or closet heater

In a garage, water runs across the slab toward the lowest point and can reach the walls and any storage on the floor. In an interior closet, it often wicks into the carpet and drywall around the door.

Attic installations send water through the ceiling below, and our ceiling water damage repair page covers that second stage. For a heater inside a living area, the first job is extraction, which you can read about on water extraction.

The cleanup after the tank is shut off

Water from a clean supply is Category 1 when it first escapes. ANSI/IICRC S500 uses that label for water from a sanitary supply. Waiting turns it into a bigger problem as it soaks materials. We extract the standing water, remove wet baseboards or cut drywall where it wicked, and set air movers and dehumidifiers.

Cardboard boxes, stored fabric and anything porous on the floor is usually discarded if it sat in the water. Rigid items on shelves are wiped and kept.

Cost factors for water heater leak cleanup

Costs depend on how long the tank ran. Five factors matter most.

  • Gallons released and how far they traveled.
  • Floor type, since slab is simple and carpet or wood is not.
  • Whether drywall or insulation must be cut out.
  • Drying days with dehumidifiers and air movers.
  • Rebuild work after the wet materials are removed.

Preventing the next failure

Replace a tank when it is near the end of its expected life, and install a drain pan with a drain line where the heater sits above living space. A leak sensor on the floor costs little and gives an early warning.
