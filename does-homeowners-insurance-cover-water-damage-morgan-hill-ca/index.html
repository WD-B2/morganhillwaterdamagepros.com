Homeowners Insurance and Water Damage Coverage in Morgan Hill, CA

Sometimes yes, sometimes no. The cause of the water decides almost everything, and a few exclusions catch Morgan Hill homeowners off guard.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

A standard homeowners policy usually covers water damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a burst pipe or a failed appliance hose. It usually does not cover flood, groundwater seepage, or damage from slow leaks and poor maintenance. Sewer backup is often excluded unless you added an endorsement.

That is the short answer, and your own policy wording is the final word. This guide explains the common patterns so you know what to ask before you file. It is general information, not legal or insurance advice.

Sudden and accidental versus gradual

Insurers draw a line between a loss that happens quickly and one that develops over time. A pipe that bursts on Tuesday night is sudden. A drain that dripped behind a wall for six months is gradual, and policies commonly exclude it.

The distinction matters for mold too. Mold that follows a covered sudden loss may be handled differently from mold that grew from a long-term leak. Policies often cap mold coverage, so read that section before you assume.

Flood needs its own policy

Flood sits outside standard homeowners and renters coverage. Separate NFIP policies exist, but they typically start after a 30-day wait. A policy bought once a storm is forecast will not help with that storm.

If your community takes part in the Community Rating System, eligible policyholders get a discount. Ask your agent whether your address qualifies, and look up your parcel on the FEMA flood map.

Sewer backup and sump failures

A backed-up drain or a failed sump pump is a different peril from a burst pipe. Many policies exclude it, and an endorsement can add limited coverage. Ask your agent for the limit in dollars, since endorsements are often capped well below the cost of a serious loss.

If sewage entered the house, the cleanup follows contamination rules and costs more than a clean-water loss. Our sewage backup cleanup page explains what the work involves.

What to do before you call the insurer

The California Department of Insurance advises homeowners to protect the property from further damage, keep receipts and photograph the loss. Report water damage immediately, and hold off on permanent repairs until the adjuster has assessed the damage.

Emergency steps such as shutting off water and extracting standing water are expected, and reasonable costs are part of a typical claim. A restoration company can document moisture readings and equipment logs that support your file. Our water extraction team keeps those records on every job.

Questions to ask your agent today

Write the answers down. A five-minute call now is easier than a long dispute later.

  • Is my policy written on a replacement-cost or actual-cash-value basis?
  • What is my deductible for water damage, and is it a flat amount or a percentage?
  • What are the limits and exclusions for mold?
  • Do I have any sewer or drain backup endorsement, and what is the limit?
  • Is flood excluded, and what would a separate policy cost at my address?

Maintenance, wear and the exclusions that surprise people

Most policies exclude damage that results from wear, rust, rot or lack of maintenance. A supply line that corroded through over years is a wear problem, even though the water that escaped was sudden. Insurers sometimes pay for the damage the water caused but not for replacing the failed part.

That split catches homeowners off guard. The plumber's bill for the pipe may be yours, while the drywall, flooring and drying may be claimable. Ask your adjuster to explain which part of the loss falls where.

Also look for clauses about unoccupied homes. Many policies limit water damage coverage if a house sits vacant for a set period, and a frozen or burst pipe in an empty house is the classic example.

Deductibles and small losses

A deductible is the amount you pay before coverage starts. Some policies use a flat dollar figure, and others use a percentage of the home's insured value. A small leak that costs less than your deductible may not be worth filing.

Filing a claim can also affect your premium or renewal, depending on the insurer. Ask your agent how they treat a small water claim before you decide. Some homeowners pay for minor repairs themselves and save the policy for large losses.

Whatever you decide, document the loss. If a hidden problem appears later, your photos and invoices show when the damage first occurred.

How restoration records support a claim

The adjuster will want proof of what happened and how long it lasted. A restoration crew can supply moisture readings, equipment logs and photos that show the extent of the loss and the steps taken to limit it.

Those records do not decide coverage. They do give the adjuster facts, which tends to speed the process. Our structural drying team logs readings at every visit for that reason.

What this means in Morgan Hill

Part of Morgan Hill sits in a FEMA high-risk flood zone, and Llagas Creek has a record of repeat flooding. Street flooding in January 2017 is a reminder that a standard homeowners policy would not have covered those losses.

Morgan Hill has been a Community Rating System participant since 2003, which earns eligible flood policyholders a 15 percent discount. If you live near Llagas or Coyote Creek, a flood quote is worth requesting. Our Coyote and San Martin pages describe how creeks and wells shape risk in those communities.
