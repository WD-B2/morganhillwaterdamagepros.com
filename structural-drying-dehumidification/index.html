Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Morgan Hill, CA

Surface-dry is not dry. We measure moisture inside walls and floors, then run equipment until the readings say the structure is dry.

Call (844) 950-6900All services

Structural drying in Morgan Hill removes the moisture that soaks into walls, floors and framing after water is gone from the surface. A house can feel dry and still hold water inside drywall, insulation and subfloor. Call (844) 950-6900 and we will schedule a moisture mapping visit.

Water that stays inside materials leads to swelling, warping, odor and mold. Drying with the right equipment and measurement is what stops that chain.

The wall feels dry but the room smells musty

Most homeowners search for what they notice, not for the trade name. They notice walls that still feel cool, a floor that will not stop feeling damp, baseboards that are swelling, or a musty smell that returns after cleaning. They notice fans running for days with no change.

Those symptoms point to water still held inside materials. Structural drying is the method that finds that water and removes it. It uses moisture meters, thermal imaging, air movers and dehumidifiers, and it follows the drying standard in ANSI/IICRC S500.

How moisture mapping works

Technicians scan with a thermal camera to spot cool, wet areas. They confirm with a pin meter and a non-invasive meter, and they write down readings on a map of the room. The readings set a baseline and mark the extent of the wet zone.

Each visit repeats the readings. If a wall stops losing moisture, the plan changes. Drying is a measured process, not a timer on a machine.

Equipment and what each piece does

Air movers push air across wet surfaces so moisture evaporates. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air so it does not settle back. Low-grain refrigerant units work well in cool, damp air, and desiccant units suit very low humidity.

Injection drying systems push warm air into wall cavities and under floors through small ports. This lets us dry hardwood and walls without removing them when conditions allow.

How long structural drying takes

It depends on the material, the amount of water, and the weather inside the house. Drywall dries faster than framing, and framing dries faster than a thick subfloor. Morgan Hill's dry summers help, though wet winter weeks slow drying.

Equipment stays until the structure reads dry for its material. We log readings on every visit so you see the progress. Premature removal of equipment is the main cause of recurring odor and mold.

When mold shows up during drying

If growth is found, drying continues, but containment and removal begin as well. Our mold remediation crew follows ANSI/IICRC S520 for that step.

Drying is also the first step after extraction, as described on water extraction, and it follows most losses from burst pipes and floods.

What to do during drying as a homeowner

Keep doors and windows closed unless the crew says otherwise. Open windows let humid outdoor air in and slow drying. Do not turn off the equipment, and plan around the noise.

Keep a simple log of the equipment you see running each day. Note any new odors or stains. These small notes make follow-up visits more useful.

Avoid moving the air movers. Their position is set to push air along the wet surface, and a shift can leave a damp patch behind.

What affects structural drying pricing

There is no fixed number until the wet area is measured.

  • Water category and how long materials stayed wet.
  • Square footage and number of affected rooms.
  • Materials that can dry in place versus those removed.
  • Number of air movers and dehumidifiers and days on site.
  • Rebuild work that follows once readings are normal.
