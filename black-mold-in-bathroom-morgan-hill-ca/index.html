Black Mold in a Bathroom: What to Do in Morgan Hill, CA

Dark spots on grout, caulk or ceiling paint are common. Whether you clean them yourself or call a crew depends on size and cause.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

Black mold in a bathroom usually grows where moisture lingers, such as shower corners, caulk lines, around the toilet base and on ceilings above the shower. A small patch on a hard surface can often be cleaned with detergent and water, then dried. Growth on drywall, behind tile or over a large area calls for a crew.

Color alone does not tell you what kind of mold you have. This guide covers the common causes, a safe cleaning method for small spots, the signs that point to a hidden leak and when to bring in help.

Why bathrooms grow mold

Bathrooms collect warm, wet air every day. If the exhaust fan is weak, off or vented into the attic, that moisture settles on cool surfaces and stays. Soap film and body oils give mold something to feed on.

Slow plumbing leaks add to the problem. A dripping supply line or a cracked shower pan wets drywall from inside, and the visible spot is just the edge of a larger wet area.

Surface mold or a hidden leak?

The place where the mold appears gives clues. Growth in caulk, grout and the lower corners of a shower points to humidity. Growth that returns right after cleaning, or a stain that spreads on the ceiling, points to a leak.

Cleaning a small patch safely

The EPA says that if the moldy area is less than about 10 square feet, a homeowner can usually handle the cleanup. Hard, non-porous surfaces can be scrubbed with detergent and water and dried thoroughly. Larger jobs fall under ANSI/IICRC S520, which our mold remediation crews follow.

Wear gloves, eye protection and a mask. Do not mix cleaning products, and keep the room ventilated. Porous materials such as ceiling tile and carpet may have to be discarded if they become moldy. That follows EPA guidance for cleaning mold at home.

  1. Dry the area. Run the fan and open a window.
  2. Scrub with detergent and water. Work in small sections.
  3. Rinse and dry fully. A towel and fan help.
  4. Watch for return. If growth returns within a week or two, moisture is still there.

When to call a crew

Call if the area is larger than about 10 square feet, if mold is in drywall or insulation or if you see a stain growing. Call too if the mold followed a flood or sewage backup. Water that has been present for days needs measurement.

Our mold remediation team contains the area, removes affected materials and dries the structure. We do not make health claims, and a physician is the right person for symptom questions.

Fixing the cause

Fixing the cause matters more than any cleaner. Without it, the dark spots return.

  • Run the exhaust fan during and for 20 minutes after showers.
  • Check that the fan vents outside, not into the attic.
  • Squeegee glass and tile after use.
  • Replace cracked caulk and failed grout.
  • Repair drips at faucets and under the vanity.

Telling mold from soap scum and mildew stains

Not every dark mark is mold. Soap scum is chalky and wipes away easily. Mildew is a surface growth that appears on damp shower curtains and tile and often cleans with detergent. Mold can be fuzzy, speckled or slimy and usually returns where moisture lingers.

A simple test is to scrub a small area with detergent and water. If the mark lifts and does not return in a week, it was likely surface staining. If it comes back quickly or leaves a stain in the material, moisture is still present.

Color does not identify the species. Black, green and gray growth can all appear in the same bathroom, and the fix is the same, which is to clean what is cleanable, dry the space and remove the moisture source.

Exhaust fans: the cheapest fix

A fan that runs briefly and vents into the attic leaves most of the moisture behind. Check that the duct runs outside, that the flap opens when the fan runs and that the fan moves air. Hold a tissue near the grille. If it does not hold, the fan is weak.

Run the fan during every shower and for about 20 minutes after. A timer switch makes that easy. In a bathroom with no fan, crack a window and leave the door open after showers.

A dehumidifier in a nearby hall helps in humid weeks. The aim is simple, which is to move wet air out before it settles.

Products people ask about

Many store-bought sprays promise to kill mold. The EPA's advice for hard surfaces is simpler, which is detergent and water, followed by thorough drying. Strong chemicals can irritate lungs and eyes, and they do not remove the moisture that feeds growth.

Never mix bleach with ammonia or acids. The fumes are dangerous, and a small bathroom concentrates them. If you use any product, follow the label, ventilate and wear protection.

Mold-resistant paint and caulk are useful after cleaning, but they do not replace ventilation and leak repair. Think of them as a second line of defense.

What this means in Morgan Hill

Morgan Hill's city water is hard, with a 2021 reported average near 302 mg/L, so scale and soap film build up quickly on tile and glass. A mineral film can make surfaces look dirty and can hide early growth, so scrub with detergent first and look again.

Homes in the foothills around Paradise Valley and older houses near downtown often have bathrooms on exterior walls, which stay cooler and collect more condensation in winter.
