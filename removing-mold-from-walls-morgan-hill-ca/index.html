Removing Mold From Walls in Morgan Hill, CA

Painted drywall hides what is behind it. Learn when a wipe-down is enough and when the wall has to come open.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

To remove mold from a wall, first find and fix the moisture source, then clean small patches on painted, hard-surfaced wall with detergent and water and dry the area thoroughly. If the drywall is soft, stained through, wallpapered or the patch is larger than about 10 square feet, the wall should be opened and the affected board removed.

Drywall is paper-faced gypsum, which soaks up water and feeds mold. That is why a quick wipe often fails. The sections below show how to tell the difference.

Surface growth or growth inside the wall?

Press the area and look at the edges. Firm paint with spotty dark patches may be surface growth from humidity. A soft spot, bubbling paint or a stain that keeps growing means the board is wet inside.

A moisture meter settles the question. A reading above normal behind a clean-looking wall means water is present, and the wall deserves attention before it becomes a bigger problem.

What drywall can and cannot recover from

Drywall that stayed wet for a short time and shows only surface growth can sometimes be cleaned. Drywall with growth through the paper face, or that stayed wet for days, is cut out.

What to do for a small patch, safely

The EPA says homeowners can usually clean up areas under about 10 square feet. If someone in the home has health concerns, talk with a physician before starting.

  1. Fix the source. Repair the leak or reduce the humidity.
  2. Protect yourself. Gloves, eye protection and a mask.
  3. Clean with detergent and water. Do not soak the wall.
  4. Dry quickly. Use a dehumidifier and a fan.
  5. Check again in two weeks. Return of growth means moisture remains.

When the wall has to be opened

A professional cuts out the affected drywall, removes wet insulation and dries the framing. The area is isolated with plastic so spores do not drift. Surfaces that remain are HEPA vacuumed and cleaned.

Our mold remediation page explains the process, and the repair afterward is covered on wall and drywall water damage repair. Do not paint until the wall is dry and the source is fixed.

Where wall mold comes from

  • A slow leak inside the wall from a supply line or drain.
  • Condensation on cold exterior walls in winter.
  • A roof or window leak that runs down the wall.
  • Water wicking up from a wet floor or slab.
  • An air conditioner closet or drain pan overflow.

Wallpaper, texture and paint

Wallpaper traps moisture against the wall. If you see dark spots under peeling paper, remove a small section to look at the board. Mold under wallpaper often means the wall behind is wetter than it looks.

Textured walls hold spores in their peaks and valleys, which makes surface cleaning less effective. Heavy texture on a moldy area often means removing the board rather than scrubbing.

Primers sold as mold-resistant help only after the wall is clean, dry and free of moisture. Painting over active growth hides it and does not stop it.

How long drying takes after the source is fixed

Drywall that stayed wet for a short time usually dries in a few days with air movement and a dehumidifier. Thick plaster, heavy insulation or a wall that was wet for weeks takes longer.

Take a moisture reading before painting or closing the wall. A reading that matches a dry wall nearby is a good sign. If the reading is high, wait or open the wall further.

Re-check the area after two or three weeks. New staining means water is still getting in. A fresh reading and a closer look at the likely source, such as a roof edge or a shower valve, usually pays off.

Protecting the rest of the house during cleanup

Close the door to the room and tape plastic sheeting over it. Turn off the heating and cooling system's vents in that room so air does not carry spores. Use a damp cloth rather than dry dusting.

If you cut out drywall, score the paper and remove it in sections. Fold the pieces inward and bag them at once. Vacuum the area with a HEPA vacuum afterward.

Never use a standard household vacuum on mold. It can exhaust spores into the room.

Rebuilding the wall after mold removal

Once the studs read dry and the cavity is clean, new insulation and drywall go in. Use moisture-resistant board in bathrooms and laundry rooms. Seal joints and finish with primer and paint.

Match the texture where you can. If the wall is old, expect some blending. A painter can feather the edges across a wider area to hide the patch.

Keep a dehumidifier running in the room for a few weeks to confirm that humidity stays under control.
