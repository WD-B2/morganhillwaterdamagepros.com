Emergency Water Extraction in Morgan Hill, CA

Standing water ruins flooring and drywall fast. Our dispatch line answers 24/7 and sends a crew to remove it from homes and businesses across Morgan Hill.

Call (844) 950-6900All services

Standing water in a Morgan Hill home is an emergency from the first hour. Water wicks into drywall, subfloors and cabinet bases while you read this. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, including nights and weekends, so call now and keep reading while you wait.

Water extraction is the first step of any wet-building job. A crew uses pumps and extraction units to pull bulk water off floors and out of carpet before the drying phase begins. Until the standing water is gone, nothing else can dry.

While you wait for the crew, stop the source if you can, shut off the main water valve for a pipe failure, and lift small items off the floor. Stay out of any room where water touches outlets or the panel.

When standing water is an emergency

Call right away when water covers more than a few square feet, when it has reached drywall, or when it is moving toward another room. Water travels along the path of least resistance, which is often under a wall into the next room.

Ankle-deep water in a garage or a family room is a clear emergency call. So is a soaked carpet over a pad, because the pad holds water like a sponge and keeps feeding the subfloor underneath.

If the water came from a toilet overflow, a sewer line or a creek, treat it as contaminated. Keep children and pets out, and read the sewage page before touching anything. Our sewage backup cleanup crews handle that work with different protective gear.

How a crew removes the water

The tool depends on the surface. A submersible pump clears deep water in a garage or lower level in minutes. A weighted wand extractor pulls water out of carpet and pad, and a squeegee-style floor tool works on tile and sealed wood.

Technicians then check the room with a moisture meter, since a floor that looks dry on top can stay wet below. The reading decides whether the carpet pad comes out, whether baseboards need pulling, and where drying equipment goes.

  1. Stop the source. Close the main valve or the fixture shutoff before anything else.
  2. Pump the bulk water. Submersible pumps handle depth, extractors handle what is left in soft materials.
  3. Check for hidden moisture. Meters and a thermal camera show wet drywall and subfloor that the eye misses.
  4. Hand off to drying. Air movers and dehumidifiers take over, covered on our structural drying page.

What the water category changes

The industry standard for water damage, ANSI/IICRC S500, sorts water into three categories by how contaminated it is. The category decides what can be dried in place and what must be thrown away.

Water categories and what they mean for extraction.
CategoryTypical sourceWhat usually happens
Category 1Supply line, sink overflow, rainwater from a clean roofExtract and dry in place. Carpet and pad are often saved if the work starts quickly.
Category 2Washing machine discharge, dishwasher overflow, aquariumExtract, clean and treat. Pad is often removed, carpet may be saved.
Category 3Sewage, creek flood water, long-standing waterExtract with protective gear. Porous materials are removed and disposed of.

Water that sits long enough can move up a category. Clean supply-line water left for days becomes a mold and bacteria problem, so speed matters even when the source was clean.

Wet carpet, wood and tile respond differently

Carpet and pad drink water, but the carpet itself can often be lifted, extracted and dried if the water is clean. Hardwood is harder, since boards swell and cup within days, and our hardwood floor water damage page explains how that is judged.

Tile and sealed concrete shed water easily, though the grout lines and the subfloor below can stay wet. A wet subfloor is why extraction is only the first half of the job.

What shapes the cost of an extraction job

No honest number exists until someone has measured the room. These are the factors that move the price.

  • Water category, because Category 3 work needs protective gear and disposal.
  • Square footage and depth of water, which set pump time and crew size.
  • Materials involved, with carpet pad and drywall adding removal work.
  • How long the water has been standing before the call.
  • Whether drying and rebuild work follow the extraction.
