San Martin is an unincorporated community of 7,008 people at the 2020 census, set between Morgan Hill and Gilroy. It sits outside the spheres of influence of both cities, and its elevation is about 289 feet. That mix of rural lots and open land shapes the water damage we see here.

Water damage restoration in San Martin starts with the same urgent steps as anywhere, but the cause is often different. Many homes run on private wells and septic systems, which means pump failures, supply line leaks and tank backups are common calls. Our dispatch line at (844) 950-6900 answers around the clock.

When water is spreading in San Martin

A spreading leak on a rural lot has no neighbor to notice it and no city crew to shut it off. You may be the only person who knows. If the water is coming from a well line, shut off the pump breaker and the pressure tank valve. If it is a house line, close the main inside the home.

Emergency water removal is available here at any hour. Call the 24/7 line, say where the water is and whether sewage is involved, and a crew drives down from Morgan Hill. The distance is about five miles by road, depending on where in San Martin you are.

Wells, septic and what fails

Most San Martin homes use private wells and septic systems. The San Martin County Water District serves a portion of the community east of Monterey Road, and West San Martin Waterworks serves CordeValle and Hayes Lane. Lions Gate Community Services District handles wastewater at CordeValle.

Check your water bill or your property records to learn which system you are on. A well pump that fails to shut off, a pressure tank that cracks and a septic line that backs up each produce a different kind of damage. For wastewater inside the home, read our sewage backup cleanup page, which explains why septic problems need a plumber and a cleanup crew.

If water is still spreading: before we arrive

These steps buy time on a large lot where the crew has a drive ahead.

Stop the source

Close the main valve, or switch off the well pump at its breaker. Open a low faucet to drain pressure.

Protect the power

Switch off breakers to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel dry. Call the utility for any wet panel.

Lift what you can

Raise furniture legs onto blocks and move boxes, shoes and rugs off the floor.

Keep people clear

Keep children and pets out of any water that smells of sewage or has a gray, cloudy look.
