Holiday Lake Estates is a hillside neighborhood of about 512 homes overlooking the south shore of Lake Anderson. Jackson Oaks adjoins it, and its homeowners association was incorporated in 1969. Holiday Drive and Blue Jay Drive are the main streets, and the neighborhood has a single road in and out.

Water damage restoration here has a practical wrinkle. A crew has to climb the hill, so knowing the street and the nearest cross street saves time. Call (844) 950-6900 and the dispatcher will confirm your address before sending anyone.

One road in and out

With a single access road, a blocked street or a storm closure can slow a crew. Tell dispatch about any downed branches, parked vehicles or road damage you see. If the road is closed, say so on the first call.

Night calls are handled the same way as daytime calls. Keep your driveway clear and open side gates so the crew can reach hoses and equipment without delay.

Hillside homes and how water moves

On a slope, water follows gravity. Runoff from a heavy storm can find its way toward foundations, garages and crawlspaces, and roof or gutter overflow can drop water against walls. Burst lines inside the house behave like they do anywhere, and drying plans depend on materials and moisture readings.

Building age varies by lot, so do not assume the pipe or roof type of your home. Ask your insurer or a plumber about your own home. For storm leaks, read our storm water damage page.

If water is still spreading on the hill: first steps

These steps matter most when the crew has a hill to climb.

Stop the source

Close the main water valve. If water is coming from outside, do not wade through it while the power is on.

Check the panel

Switch off breakers to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel dry. Otherwise call the utility.

Move people and pets up

Take everyone to a dry room, and keep children away from standing water.

Lift and photograph

Raise valuables off the floor and photograph the waterline and the damage before cleanup.
