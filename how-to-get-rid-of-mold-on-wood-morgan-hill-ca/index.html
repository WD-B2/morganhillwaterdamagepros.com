How to Get Rid of Mold on Wood in Morgan Hill, CA

Wood is porous, so the cure depends on how deep the growth goes. Surface patches clean up. Soaked wood often needs replacing.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

To get rid of mold on wood, dry the wood first, remove surface growth with a HEPA vacuum and a damp cloth with detergent, sand lightly if the stain remains and fix the moisture source. Sealed furniture cleans up easily, while raw framing that stayed wet may need cutting out. If the wood is soft, call a professional.

The right approach depends on what the wood is and how long it was wet. Here is how to sort it out step by step.

Why wood holds mold

Wood absorbs moisture and keeps it. Mold roots itself in the surface, and in damp wood it can reach deeper than you can see. A sealed or varnished surface resists growth, while bare wood in a crawl space does not.

Mold needs moisture, so drying comes first. Without drying, no cleaner will hold.

Is the wood furniture, trim or framing?

Different wood has different outcomes.

A careful cleaning method for small areas

The EPA says that for moldy areas under about 10 square feet, homeowners can usually do the cleanup themselves. Larger areas, or any case with a lot of water damage, call for a professional.

  1. Dry the wood. Move it to a dry, ventilated place. Run a dehumidifier.
  2. Vacuum with a HEPA vacuum. This lifts loose growth without spreading it.
  3. Scrub with detergent and water. Use a soft brush and a damp cloth, not a soaking one.
  4. Sand lightly if a stain remains. Wear a mask and eye protection, and bag the dust.
  5. Seal or finish. Only after the wood is fully dry.

Structural wood and when to call

Framing, joists and subfloor are part of the structure, so do not cut them out on your own. A professional checks moisture content and tests for softness. Wood that dries to normal and stays firm can be cleaned and kept.

Wood that crumbles when pressed, that smells strongly or that is dark all the way through is often replaced. Our mold remediation team follows ANSI/IICRC S520 for containment and removal. Drying equipment comes from our structural drying crews.

Keeping mold from coming back

  • Fix leaks and condensation sources.
  • Keep crawl space vents clear and soil covered with a vapor barrier.
  • Raise wood off damp concrete and soil.
  • Use a dehumidifier in damp rooms and basements.
  • Store wood furniture away from exterior wet walls.

Furniture, decks and outdoor wood

Sealed wood furniture is the easiest case. Wipe it with a damp cloth and detergent, dry it in the sun for a short time and polish. Unsealed or antique pieces need a gentler approach, since soaking can warp joints and lift finish.

Decks and fences grow mold because they stay damp. Scrub with a stiff brush and detergent, rinse and let the wood dry fully before sealing. If boards are soft or crumble, they have lost strength and should be replaced.

Move furniture away from damp walls and out of damp basements. A few inches of air behind a piece makes a big difference.

Testing wood for moisture and softness

A pin-type moisture meter gives a reading in percent. Wood in a typical home sits in a modest range, and readings well above that mean water is present. A probe test is simple too. Press a screwdriver tip into the wood, and if it sinks in easily, the wood is soft.

Take readings at several places along a joist or beam, not just at the dark patch. Moisture often extends beyond the visible mark.

If readings drop to normal after drying and the wood is firm, cleaning is usually enough. If they stay high, the source of water is still active and needs to be found.

Protective gear and dust control

Sanding or brushing mold sends spores into the air. Wear a fitted respirator rated for fine particles, eye protection and gloves. Disposable coveralls keep spores off your clothes.

Work outdoors when possible. If you must work inside, close doors, cover vents with plastic and run a HEPA air filter. Mist the surface lightly so dust stays down, but do not soak.

Bag debris in thick plastic and seal the bag before carrying it through the house. Wash your hands and face afterward, and launder clothes separately.

When replacement beats cleaning

Replace wood that is soft, deeply stained or full of fine cracks that hold growth. Replacement is often cheaper than repeated cleaning, and it gives a solid surface.

For structural members, consult a professional before cutting. A carpenter or engineer can say whether a joist can be sistered, which means attaching a new board alongside it, instead of replaced.

Older timber buildings downtown and in the original townsite have long-lived framing that has seen many seasons of leaks. Villa Mira Monte, built in 1884, is a reminder of how old some local lumber is. Old wood can be sound, but it needs a meter check after any water event.
