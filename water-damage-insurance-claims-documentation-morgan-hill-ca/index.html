Water Damage Insurance Claim Documentation in Morgan Hill, CA

A good file makes a claim move faster. Here is what to photograph, save and write down, in the order it matters.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

To document a water damage claim, take wide and close photos before you clean, list every damaged item, keep every receipt and save the moisture readings from the crew drying your home. Report the loss to your insurer immediately and keep damaged items until the adjuster says otherwise.

The steps below follow guidance in the California Department of Insurance residential property claims guide. They are general information, and your policy and adjuster decide the details.

Start with safety, then protect the property

Stop the source, turn off power to wet areas only if it is safe and move people out of danger. Policies expect you to protect the property from further damage, so emergency steps are part of the claim.

Save the receipts for anything you buy, such as plastic sheeting, towels or a hotel night. This follows the guidelines in the California Department of Insurance residential property claims guide. Reasonable emergency costs are commonly included in a claim, though the adjuster decides.

Photographs and video that help

Take more than you think you need. Storage is cheap, and a missing photo cannot be retaken.

  • Wide shots of each room from two corners before anything moves.
  • Close shots of the water source, the stains and the lowest wet point.
  • Photos of the waterline on walls, cabinets and furniture.
  • A slow video walk-through, narrating what each room contains.
  • Photos of damaged items before they are discarded, with labels or serial numbers visible.

Build an inventory as you go

List each damaged item with a brief description, the age, the approximate price and where it was. A simple spreadsheet or a notes file works. Add a column for whether you kept the item.

Hold on to damaged belongings until the adjuster releases them, as state insurance guidance suggests. If an item is hazardous and must go, photograph it first and tell the adjuster.

What the restoration crew should hand you

Ask for the written scope, the daily moisture readings, the equipment list with days on site and the photos the crew took. These records show how much water was involved and how long drying took.

Written bids and licensed contractors

The California Department of Insurance guide advises getting written bids from licensed contractors. Compare scope, not only price, since two bids can describe different work.

A repair job at or above $1,000 in labor and materials needs a licensed contractor in California. Ask for the license number and check it with the state before signing.

If mold shows up during the claim

Contact the adjuster immediately if you suspect mold. Do not wait until repairs are finished. Mold limits are common in policies, and a late report can complicate the claim.

Our mold remediation page explains how growth is contained and removed. Questions about symptoms belong with a physician, not a restoration crew.

A simple timeline to keep

Write down the sequence from the first moment you noticed water. Notes made on the day are more reliable than memory a month later. Short entries are enough.

  • Date and time you discovered the water and what you saw.
  • When you shut off the source and who you called.
  • When the restoration crew arrived and what they did.
  • When you reported the loss and the claim number you were given.
  • Every conversation with the adjuster, including the name, date and what was said.

Keep the timeline with your photos in a single folder, on paper or in the cloud. A well-kept timeline answers most of the questions an adjuster asks at the first call.

Contents versus structure

Claims usually split into two parts. The structure covers walls, floors, ceilings and built-ins. Contents cover furniture, clothing, electronics and personal items. Each has its own list and often its own limit.

Build both lists as you clean. For structure, note the room, the material and the approximate size of the damage. For contents, note the item, the age and the cost. Add receipts or screenshots of the purchase if you have them.

Ask your adjuster whether your policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value. The difference changes how you value each item.

Mistakes that slow a claim

Each of these is avoidable. A short checklist taped to the fridge helps in the stressful first days of a loss.

  • Throwing away damaged items before they are photographed or approved.
  • Starting permanent repairs before the adjuster has assessed the damage.
  • Waiting to report a suspected mold problem.
  • Losing receipts for emergency purchases.
  • Signing a contractor's authorization form without reading what it assigns.

Working with the adjuster

Be present for the adjuster's visit if you can. Walk them through each room in the same order as your photos. Point out the source of the water, the lowest wet point and any damage that is not obvious from the surface.

Ask what they need to see and what happens next. Take notes on their answers. If they promise a follow-up, write down the date and check in if it passes.

If the adjuster's estimate looks low or misses items, say so politely and send your documents. Disagreements are common and often resolve with better records.

What this means in Morgan Hill

Structural and plumbing repairs inside city limits go through the permit counter, but cosmetic finishes do not. Ask the city office about your scope and keep any permit paperwork in your claim file.

If your home sits near a creek, record the waterline from outside as well, since flood claims are handled differently from pipe claims. Our San Jose and Coyote pages cover permit and flood context for nearby areas.

Claim documentation questions

Report it immediately. The California Department of Insurance advises prompt reporting, and your policy may have its own notice requirements.

Do the emergency steps that prevent more damage, and photograph everything first. Keep damaged items until the adjuster says otherwise.

Hold off on permanent repairs until the adjuster has assessed the damage. Temporary measures that prevent more loss are expected.

Policies and practices vary. Ask your insurer what is required, and get written bids from licensed contractors for comparison.

Start with your insurer in writing. The California Department of Insurance consumer hotline is 1-800-927-4357.
