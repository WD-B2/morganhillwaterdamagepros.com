What to Keep, Clean or Throw Away After Water Damage in Morgan Hill, CA

The answer depends on the material and the type of water. This guide sorts a wet house into keep, clean and discard.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

After water damage, keep hard, non-porous items such as glass, metal and sealed plastic and clean them. Throw away porous items that soaked in contaminated water, including carpet pad, mattresses, upholstered furniture and particleboard. Items touched by clean water for a short time can often be dried and saved. Photograph everything before you discard it.

Two questions decide each item. First, what was the water, clean, gray or contaminated. Second, is the item porous or hard. The table below sorts the most common things.

Start with the type of water

Clean water from a supply line is the easiest case. Gray water from a washing machine or dishwasher carries soap and soil. Black water, which includes sewage and floodwater, carries bacteria and should be treated as contaminated.

Time also matters. Clean water that sits for days can pick up bacteria and mold, and moves up a category. Quick action keeps more of your belongings.

Food, medicine and kitchenware

Public health agencies advise discarding food and drinks that touched floodwater or sewage. That includes items in packages that are not waterproof. Refrigerated food that lost power for too long should also go.

Hard kitchenware such as metal pots and glass dishes can usually be washed and sanitized. Wooden cutting boards and porous utensils that soaked in contaminated water are usually discarded. Medicine that got wet should be replaced, and a pharmacist can advise.

Documents, photos and keepsakes

Wet papers and photos can often be saved if handled fast. Do not toss them yet. The rule of thumb is to keep them cool and flat and to move quickly.

For items of high value, an appraiser or conservator can advise before you decide. Take photos first.

Document before you discard

Keep damaged items until your adjuster has looked at them.

  • Photograph each item and note a brand, age and cost.
  • Keep a sample of carpet or fabric when you discard the rest.
  • Put discarded items in a single place, not a landfill run, until the adjuster has seen them.
  • Save receipts for replacements and emergency purchases.
  • Tell the adjuster before you throw away anything large.

Structure versus contents

Your belongings are one side of the job. The building is the other. Wet drywall, insulation and flooring are handled by a crew, and our structural drying page explains how that works. If sewage is involved, see sewage backup cleanup.

Cleaning hard items safely

Do not mix cleaning products. Chemicals combined can give off dangerous fumes, and a small bathroom makes it worse.

  1. Wear protection. Gloves, boots and eye protection for any item that touched dirty water.
  2. Remove loose dirt. Hose or wipe off mud and debris outdoors if you can.
  3. Wash with detergent and water. Scrub all surfaces, including crevices.
  4. Sanitize if needed. Use a household sanitizer as the label directs on non-porous items.
  5. Dry completely. Air dry in a ventilated space before storing.

When it is worth hiring a contents service

Some items are costly or sentimental, such as fine furniture, artwork, instruments and electronics. A professional contents service can clean, dry and sometimes restore them better than home methods.

Ask what the service will do, what it will charge and whether your insurer will pay. Get the answers in writing before moving valuable items.

For irreplaceable items, a specialist such as a conservator is the right person. They can advise on whether treatment is possible before anything is attempted.

Furniture and soft goods in detail

Solid wood furniture with a sealed finish tolerates clean water best. Wipe it dry and let it air out away from heat. Veneered pieces and particleboard swell and lose their shape, and they rarely recover.

Upholstered pieces hold moisture inside the foam. A short soak in clean water may be treatable by a professional cleaner. Anything that sat in dirty water is usually discarded.

Mattresses, pillows and cushions are hard to dry. Even clean-water soaking often leaves them damp deep inside, which invites mold. Replacement is usually the safer choice.

Children's items and things that touch the mouth

Be stricter with toys, bottles, pacifiers and anything a child puts in their mouth. If these touched contaminated water, discard them. Hard plastic toys that touched clean water can be washed and sanitized.

Stuffed animals and fabric toys can sometimes be washed hot, but ones that soaked in dirty water are best replaced. Take a photo of favorites first, since a child may ask where they went.

What this means in Morgan Hill

Floodwater in Morgan Hill, such as the water that reached Llagas Road and Hale Avenue on January 8, 2017, comes from creeks and streets. It counts as contaminated, so porous belongings that sat in it are usually discarded. Sealed items can be washed and kept.

Residents in creek-side communities such as Coyote and rural parcels near San Martin should keep a small stash of heavy-duty bags, gloves and boots before storm season.
