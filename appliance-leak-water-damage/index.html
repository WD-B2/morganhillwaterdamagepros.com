Emergency Appliance Leak Water Damage in Morgan Hill, CA

A failed hose or drain line can flood a laundry room or kitchen in an hour. Shut the appliance valve and call our 24/7 line.

An appliance leak in Morgan Hill is an emergency when water reaches the floor and keeps coming. A washing machine, dishwasher or refrigerator line can send water across a room before anyone notices. Our dispatch line answers 24/7, so call at any hour after you shut the appliance valve.

Unplug the appliance only if the outlet is dry. Close the supply valve behind the unit, or shut off the main if you cannot find it. Mop up with towels and move rugs and boxes off the wet floor.

We extract the water and dry the floor, cabinet bases and walls. Many appliance leaks are hidden under flooring, which is why a moisture meter is part of every visit.

Appliance leak emergency: what to do and what to shut off

Different appliances shut off in different places.

  1. Washing machine. Close both hot and cold valves behind it, or pull the hose supply valve.
  2. Dishwasher. Close the small valve under the kitchen sink, then switch off its breaker.
  3. Refrigerator or ice maker. Find the saddle valve or shutoff on the line behind the unit.
  4. Unknown source. Close the main valve and call dispatch.

Where appliance leaks come from

Washing machine supply hoses are a leading cause, especially older rubber ones that bulge and crack. Dishwasher door seals and drain lines wear out. Refrigerator ice-maker lines split quietly behind the unit.

The common thread is water that escapes where nobody looks. A dishwasher leaking into a cabinet or a fridge line leaking under a floor can run for days. If you notice a swollen cabinet base or a soft spot in vinyl, assume water is underneath.

Hidden water under flooring and cabinets

Water from a kitchen appliance often runs under flooring toward a wall. Luxury vinyl and laminate trap water between layers, and wood floors cup within days. Our kitchen water damage restoration page covers cabinets and sink-base damage in more detail.

Laundry rooms usually have vinyl or tile over a plywood subfloor. Under ANSI/IICRC S500 guidelines, the drying goal is a documented reading, not a time on the clock. A wet subfloor swells and softens, so we probe it with a meter and cut out sections that will not dry.

Drying a floor after an appliance leak

Crews extract standing water first. Then air movers push air under the flooring through small ports, and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room. Cabinet toe-kicks are removed to let the base dry.

If the water was clean when it escaped, many floors can be saved with fast drying. Carpet and pad are the exception when they stayed wet for a day or more. The carpet water damage page explains when carpet can be dried in place.

What affects appliance leak repair pricing

Appliance leaks look small and often cost more than owners expect. These five factors set the price.

  • How long the leak ran before it was found.
  • Flooring type and whether it can be dried in place.
  • Cabinet and drywall removal around the appliance.
  • Number of days of drying equipment.
  • Rebuild scope for flooring, base and paint.

Cheap fixes that prevent expensive losses

Replace rubber washer hoses with braided steel ones every few years, and turn the valves off when you leave for a trip. Pull the fridge out once a year and look at the ice-maker line.

A small leak sensor on the floor is inexpensive and pays for itself on the first catch. If a leak has already reached the wall, see wall and drywall water damage repair.
