Emergency Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Morgan Hill, CA

A bulging ceiling can drop without warning. Call the 24/7 line, keep people out from under it and let a crew relieve the water safely.

A wet ceiling in Morgan Hill is an emergency when it bulges, drips or darkens. Drywall weakens as it soaks and can fall with the weight of trapped water. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so call at any hour and stay out from under the sag.

Place a bucket under any drip and move furniture away. If the ceiling is bulging, a small hole in the lowest point can let the water drain in a controlled way. Do this only if the area is clear and no lights or wiring are involved.

The leak may come from a roof, a pipe above, an AC line or an upstairs bathroom. Finding the source decides who fixes it, and we take care of the water damage either way.

Sagging ceiling emergency: what to do right now

Keep your head and your family out from under a bulge.

  1. Clear the room below. Move people, pets and furniture away from the sag.
  2. Turn off power to the area if the panel is dry. Wet light fixtures and ceiling fans are an electrical risk.
  3. Contain the drip. Use a bucket or tarp, and a screwdriver hole only at a safe, clear low point.
  4. Find the source. Check the roof, the room above and the HVAC closet.
  5. Photograph the damage. Take stained and sagging areas before anything changes.

Roof leaks and what they do to a ceiling

Roof leaks are a common cause of ceiling damage after heavy winter rain. Water enters at a flashing, a worn shingle or a skylight and travels along rafters before it shows up inside. The stain on the ceiling is often several feet from the real entry point.

A storm leak may come from the same event that caused outside damage. Our storm water damage team handles the cleanup, and a roofer should repair or tarp the opening. Roofing repair itself is not our service.

Insulation above the ceiling holds water and keeps the drywall wet long after the rain stops. That is why a ceiling that looks dry on top may still need opening.

Plumbing, AC and bathroom leaks from above

A second-floor bathroom, a supply line in the attic or a clogged AC drain pan can all wet the ceiling below. These leaks are often slow and run for weeks. The first sign is usually a tan ring that grows after each use.

If the stain follows a bathroom schedule, the leak is likely in a drain or shower pan. If it follows the air conditioner, read our page on AC leak water damage. If a pipe has burst above, see burst pipe water damage.

How a wet ceiling is repaired

Technicians measure moisture in the ceiling and walls, then cut out wet drywall so the cavity can dry. Insulation that is soaked is removed, and framing is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Once readings return to normal, new drywall is hung, taped and textured. Paint is matched where possible. Textured ceilings take extra care, since a patch must blend into the pattern around it. According to ANSI/IICRC S500 guidance, saturated insulation above a ceiling is generally removed rather than dried.

What shapes ceiling repair pricing

The visible stain rarely tells the whole story. These factors set the price.

  • Source and water category, since roof rain and pipe water differ.
  • Area of drywall and insulation that must be removed.
  • Ceiling height and texture type.
  • Drying time and equipment inside the cavity.
  • Paint and texture matching on the finished ceiling.
