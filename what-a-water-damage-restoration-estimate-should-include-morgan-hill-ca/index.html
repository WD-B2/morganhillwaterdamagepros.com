What a Water Damage Estimate Should Include in Morgan Hill, CA

A good estimate is specific. Here are the line items to expect and the red flags that call for questions.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

A water damage restoration estimate should list the water category, the affected areas with square footage, the services to be done, the equipment and the number of days, any materials to be removed and a statement about what is not included. It should separate emergency work from rebuild work, and it should explain how changes will be priced.

We do not publish prices here, because a price without measurements is a guess. This guide shows what a well-built estimate contains so you can compare bids on equal terms.

Core line items

Most estimates group the work into a few categories.

Typical estimate sections.
SectionWhat it should sayWhy it matters
Scope and areasRooms, square footage, water categorySets the size of the job.
Emergency servicesExtraction, pumping, containmentThe first-day work.
Drying equipmentAir movers and dehumidifiers with daysLargest variable cost.
DemolitionDrywall, flooring, insulation removedDefines what is thrown away.
Cleaning and treatmentAntimicrobial, deodorizingNeeded for gray and black water.
MonitoringVisits and moisture readingsProves drying is complete.
ReconstructionRebuild scope, often separatePrevents surprise add-ons.

Equipment days are where bids differ

Drying equipment is usually priced by the unit per day. A bid with fewer units may be cheaper on paper and slower in practice. Ask how many air movers and dehumidifiers are planned and why.

Ask also how the days will be decided. According to ANSI/IICRC S500, the answer should be documented moisture readings. The right answer is moisture readings, not a calendar. Our structural drying page explains how equipment is placed and removed.

Red flags in an estimate

None of these proves a problem, but each deserves a question before you sign.

  • A single lump sum with no breakdown.
  • No mention of water category or how it was judged.
  • Equipment listed without unit counts or days.
  • Pressure to sign before anyone has measured moisture.
  • No statement about what is excluded.

Emergency work versus rebuild work

Many estimates separate the two. Emergency work happens in the first days. Rebuild follows once readings are normal. Splitting them lets you approve the first stage quickly and shop for the second if you wish.

Check whether the estimate includes paint, trim and flooring, or stops at the bare drywall. Our wall and drywall water damage repair page shows what finish work looks like.

Insurance and estimate matching

If a claim is open, your adjuster writes a separate estimate. Differences are normal. Share the contractor's documents, including moisture logs and photos, and ask which line items the adjuster accepted or questioned.

The California Department of Insurance advises getting written bids from licensed contractors. Keep copies of every bid.

Contents handling and pack-out

If belongings must be moved, the estimate should say who moves them, where they go and how they are cleaned. A pack-out service boxes and stores items offsite while the room is dried and rebuilt.

Ask whether contents handling is included or priced separately, and whether it is covered by your policy. Make a list of items moved so nothing goes missing.

Ask also about storage fees and how long items can stay. Dates matter for budget and for getting back into the room.

Taxes, fees and minimums

Some estimates include minimum charges for equipment or travel. Others add fees for disposal. Ask what the totals include and whether any line items may grow once work begins.

A good estimate gives an order of magnitude for each stage and explains how a change would be priced. If a crew finds more damage, you should see the change in writing before you approve it.

Compare the payment terms as well. Ask when payments are due and whether they depend on insurer approval.

How crews measure to build an estimate

A crew starts with a walk-through. They identify the source, check moisture with meters and a thermal camera, and measure each affected room. They note the flooring, wall materials and any contamination.

That information drives the scope. Without measurements, an estimate is a guess, which is why we do not quote prices by phone. Ask any company how they measured.

A short moisture map attached to the estimate is a good sign. It shows where the wet areas are and why equipment is placed where it is.

Payment and timing

Ask when payment is due and how it is tied to milestones. Many jobs bill in stages, such as emergency services, drying and rebuild. Some companies work directly with insurers, and others ask for payment from you.

If your insurer is involved, ask who signs the direction to pay. Read it before signing. Keep copies of every signed document, and ask for a final invoice that matches the scope.

What this means in Morgan Hill

Ask the estimate whether a permit is needed. The City of Morgan Hill requires one for building repairs and alterations, with finish work such as painting and carpeting exempt. In San Jose, the Permit Center decides, and in unincorporated places such as San Martin, the county does.

A bid should also name the license holder. Jobs of $1,000 or more are not for unlicensed crews. See our San Martin page for county context.

For a South San Jose address, see our San Jose page and ask whether the estimate allows for city permits. Access and travel should also appear as plain line items.
