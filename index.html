Services

Water damage repair and restoration services

Every job starts with stopping the source, removing standing water and measuring what is wet. These are the services Morgan Hill homeowners and businesses call us for most.

Flood and storm water

Creek and street flooding is treated as contaminated, so flood damage cleanup includes removal of porous materials and disinfecting. Roof and window leaks during rain fall under storm water damage.

Emergency water removal

Pumps and extraction units pull standing water out of floors and carpet in emergency water extraction calls. The drying that follows is explained under structural drying and dehumidification.

Commercial losses

Shops, offices, restaurants and tasting rooms lose revenue every day they are wet. Our commercial water damage crews plan drying around your business.

Pipes and plumbing

A failed line can release gallons a minute, which is why burst pipe water damage calls go to the front of the queue. Cold-night splits are covered under frozen pipe water damage.

Ceilings and roofs

A stain that spreads or a ceiling that sags needs fast attention. See ceiling water damage repair, which also covers roof leaks.

Sewage and mold

Wastewater needs protective gear and disinfecting, as explained on sewage backup cleanup. When damp materials grow mold, our mold remediation page explains containment and removal.

Appliance failures, slow leaks and hidden water are just as common. Washers, dishwashers and refrigerators are covered on appliance leak water damage, and a tank that fails is handled through water heater leak cleanup. For a leak in the cooling system, see AC leak water damage.

Under the house or under the floor, water goes unseen. We cover slab leak water damage, crawl space water damage and basement water damage. For finishes, see kitchen water damage, hardwood floors, carpet and wall and drywall repair. The full list sits on our services page.

Why Morgan Hill homes get water damage

Morgan Hill sits on the valley floor between the foothills and Monterey Road, with creeks running through town. The city's Water Division pumps groundwater from the Coyote and Llagas aquifers, and its 2021 consumer confidence report lists average hardness near 302 mg/L, about 18 grains per gallon. Scale from hard water builds up in water heaters and fittings, so aging tanks and supply lines are a recurring cause of calls.

Flooding is the other major cause. About one-twelfth of the city lies in a FEMA high-risk zone, and nine named creeks cross town, with Llagas and Coyote the best known. Valley Water counts six floods on Llagas Creek since 2000.

Storm years homeowners still remember

On January 8, 2017, flooding reached Llagas Road, Main Street, Hale Avenue and Watsonville Road. Six weeks later a failed Coyote Canal levee near Cochrane Road put northbound US-101 under water. Those events show how quickly a normal winter turns into a cleanup problem.

The final phase of the Upper Llagas Creek project, running through 2027, is expected to protect about 1,100 homes and 500 businesses. New culverts at several downtown crossings are part of the plan. Until it is finished, check the FEMA flood map for your address and find out where the city hands out sandbags.

Neighborhoods and places we work around

Downtown Morgan Hill follows Monterey Road and Main Avenue, a layout that dates to C.H. Phillips dividing the rancho in 1892. Madrone, annexed in the late 1950s, lies to the north near Capriano Park, and Tennant grew up around the 21-Mile House stagecoach stop.

The Paradise Valley and Chesbro Lake area sits in the foothills west of town. Villa Mira Monte, built in 1884 for the Hill family, is one of the older estates in town. Community Park, Ann Sobrato High School and the Caltrain station give crews landmarks for directions.

Malaguerra Winery, founded in 1869, reminds us that Santa Clara Valley wineries and tasting rooms are part of the local commercial mix. Their cellars and barrel rooms bring moisture challenges of their own, which our commercial team understands.

Permits, licensing and who does what

The City of Morgan Hill's Building and Fire Prevention office, at 17575 Peak Avenue, requires a permit for any new building, addition, alteration, repair or change in use. Painting, wallpapering, carpeting and similar finish work are exempt. California's contractor licensing board requires a license when a permit is needed, when there is employee labor or when labor and materials total $1,000 or more.

Ask for the scope in writing before work starts. Our team explains the plan and the price before any rebuild begins, and we coordinate with plumbers and roofers who fix the source.

Water damage and insurance

Sudden pipe and appliance failures are usually covered by standard policies, while flood is not. The National Flood Insurance Program usually has a 30-day waiting period, and Morgan Hill has taken part in the Community Rating System since 2003, which earns policyholders a 15 percent discount.

The state insurance department's advice is simple: stop further damage, save receipts, take photos and let the adjuster look before permanent repairs. Its consumer hotline is 1-800-927-4357. We do not decide coverage, and your policy governs.
