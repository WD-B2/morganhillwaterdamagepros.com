Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Morgan Hill, CA

Drywall soaks water like a sponge and holds it inside the wall. We open, dry and rebuild walls so stains and mold do not return.

Call (844) 950-6900All services

Wall and drywall water damage in Morgan Hill shows as stains, bubbles, soft spots or swollen baseboards. Drywall wicks water upward and holds it, and the framing and insulation inside can stay wet for weeks. Call (844) 950-6900 and we will schedule a crew to check and dry the wall.

If water is still coming in, shut off the source first. Do not paint or caulk over a damp wall, because that traps moisture and hides the problem.

How to tell drywall is truly wet

Press the wall lightly. Soft or crumbling drywall is wet. Bubbling paint, brown rings and warped baseboards point the same way. A musty smell is another clue, even without a stain.

Moisture inside a wall often extends well beyond the visible stain. A meter reading across the wall shows the true size of the wet zone, and a thermal camera can find it behind paint.

Dry in place or cut it out

Drywall that got wet from clean water for a short time can sometimes be dried in place with air movers and small ports. Drywall that was soaked, stained or contaminated is cut out. Insulation inside a wet wall is removed.

A common practice is to cut the board somewhat above the visible waterline, based on meter readings. According to ANSI/IICRC S500, drywall that stays saturated or was contaminated is removed. That allows the cavity to dry and exposes any hidden wetness. Our structural drying team monitors until the framing reads dry.

Repairing the wall

After drying, new drywall is hung, taped and textured. The surface is primed and painted. Matching texture across a patch takes skill, especially on older homes with a heavier finish.

Baseboards that swelled are replaced, and cabinets or built-ins against the wall are checked. If the stain came from the ceiling above, the ceiling water damage repair page explains that path.

Water damage on baseboards and lower walls

Baseboards are often the first sign of a problem. They swell, warp and pull away from the wall when water wicks up from the floor. The source may be an appliance, a slab or a leak in a nearby wall.

If the cause is an appliance, see appliance leak water damage. If a pipe burst inside the wall, read burst pipe water damage.

What to do before you cut or paint

Resist the urge to cut the wall open yourself. A hole in the wrong place can hit wiring or a supply line, and dust from wet drywall is unpleasant to breathe. Mark the edge of the stain with a pencil and write the date next to it.

If the mark spreads over a day or two, the source is still active. Photograph the wall each morning. That record helps your adjuster and shows us how fast the water is moving.

Move furniture a few feet away from the wall, and lift anything stored along its base. Leave the heat or cooling at a normal setting so the house does not swing between damp and warm.

Permits for wall repairs

Cutting out moldy framing or drywall counts as a repair, so a city permit may apply. Finish work such as painting does not. A licensed contractor is required once the work passes $1,000.
