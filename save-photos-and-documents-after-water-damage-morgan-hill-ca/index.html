How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Morgan Hill, CA

Wet paper can often be saved if you move fast. Follow archival guidance on air drying, freezing and what not to touch.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

To save wet photos and documents, keep them from drying in a stuck-together pile, separate them gently, blot with clean absorbent paper and air dry them flat. If you cannot air dry them within about two days, freeze them to prevent mold. Do not touch the surface of photographs, and call a conservator for items of high value.

This guidance follows archival practice from the National Archives and conservation groups. It applies to family papers, not to important legal originals, which you should replace with certified copies where needed.

Act within the first two days

Mold can start on wet paper quickly. Archival guidance says items that cannot be air dried within about 48 hours should be frozen to inhibit mold growth. Freezing does not kill mold, but it holds it dormant until you can treat the items.

Wrap small bundles in freezer paper or plastic, label them and place them in a freezer. Thaw and dry them in manageable batches later.

How to air dry papers

A room with a dehumidifier speeds drying, since cool, dry air discourages mold.

  1. Clear a clean flat surface. Cover it with clean paper towels or unprinted newsprint.
  2. Separate pages gently. Work from the edge, and do not force pages that are stuck.
  3. Dry documents flat. Small piles or single sheets dry best.
  4. Change blotters as they soak. Replace towels often.
  5. Add a fan for circulation. Point it so air moves over the items, not directly at them.

Photographs need special care

Photographs can stick to each other or to glass. Dry them face up on blotting paper. Do not touch the image surface, and do not rub. If prints are stuck in a frame, dry them with the glass side down and do not force them off.

Old photographs and negatives should not be frozen according to conservation guidance. Dry them carefully or consult a conservator. Modern prints respond better to quick drying.

Mold on papers

Active mold looks fuzzy or slimy. Dormant mold is dry and powdery. Dry the items first. Do not try to wipe active mold off, since that can smear it.

Brush or vacuum dormant mold outdoors with a soft brush, and wear a mask. If a room has mold on walls, see our mold remediation page.

Replacing vital records

Some documents cannot be saved. Birth certificates, titles and passports can be replaced by the issuing agency. Make a list of what was lost and start replacements early.

Keep copies of insurance policies and ID in a cloud folder so a loss never traps you again. Your adjuster will want your policy number, so write it down somewhere dry.

Books, albums and bound volumes

Wet books are heavy and fragile. Do not open them roughly or force them shut. If they are only damp, stand them on their top edge and fan the pages out. For soaked books, interleave pages with clean blotter paper, replacing it as it soaks.

Large wet books can be frozen if you cannot dry them within about two days. Wrap each one in freezer paper first. Freezing stops mold and gives you time to plan.

Photo albums with sticky pages are harder. Separate prints only if they separate easily, and ask a conservator about the rest.

Make a safe-copy plan for next time

After a loss, scan or photograph the items you saved. A phone camera is fine for papers and prints, and a scanner is better for photos. Store copies in the cloud and on a drive kept elsewhere.

Keep a binder of originals you cannot replace in a waterproof, fire-resistant box on an upper shelf. Include copies of insurance policies, ID, vehicle titles and home records.

A little planning keeps the next leak from turning into a loss of memories. It takes an afternoon and costs little.

Fragile and special materials

Some materials need extra care. Inkjet prints can run when wet, and glossy photos stick together. Watercolors, charcoal and pastel art can bleed or lift. Do not rub or press these items.

Film, slides and video tapes are best handled by a specialist. Keep them wet in clean water if you cannot get help right away, and call a conservator.

For legal originals, such as deeds and wills, dry carefully and contact the issuing office about certified replacements. Do not assume a damaged original is useless, since some offices accept dried documents.

What this means in Morgan Hill

If you live near a creek such as Llagas or Coyote, keep irreplaceable papers and photos on an upper shelf or in a sealed tub rather than in a garage or lower cabinet. Valley Water counts six floods on Llagas Creek since 2000, and garages are the first to take water.
