Water Damage in a Rental: Morgan Hill, CA Tenants and Landlords

Both sides have jobs after a leak. Written notice, quick action and good records protect everyone.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

When water damages a rental in California, the tenant should tell the landlord in writing right away and take photos. The landlord is generally responsible for keeping the home habitable, which includes working plumbing and weatherproof roofs and walls. The tenant's renters insurance usually covers belongings, while the landlord's policy covers the building.

Quick, documented action limits damage and disputes. This is general information, not legal advice, and a tenant rights group or attorney can advise on your situation.

First steps for tenants

  1. Stop the source if you can. Close a fixture shutoff or the unit valve and tell the landlord.
  2. Notify the landlord in writing. Text or email works, and keep a copy.
  3. Photograph everything. Include the source, the damage and your belongings.
  4. Protect your belongings. Move them out of the wet area.
  5. Call your renters insurer. Report damage to personal property.

What landlords usually handle

California requires landlords to keep rentals habitable. The standard comes from California Civil Code section 1941.1. That covers items such as working plumbing and a weatherproof structure. When a pipe bursts or a roof leaks, the landlord arranges repairs and drying.

Landlords should respond promptly, hire qualified help and keep records. Tenants should allow reasonable access. California generally requires written notice before a landlord enters, with 24 hours presumed reasonable, except in emergencies.

Mold in a rental

Report mold to the landlord in writing as soon as you see it, and keep photos. Do not scrub large areas yourself. The EPA suggests that people handle only small areas, under about 10 square feet.

If growth is larger, the landlord should arrange remediation. Our mold remediation page explains the process, and a physician is the right person for any symptom questions.

For landlords: a quick action checklist

  • Respond in writing the same day.
  • Shut off the source and hire a drying crew promptly.
  • Notify your insurance carrier and keep receipts.
  • Offer temporary relief if the unit is unusable, based on your lease and counsel.
  • Document readings and repairs before closing walls.

Renters insurance basics

Renters insurance usually covers your belongings, additional living expenses and some liability. It generally does not cover the building. If a leak ruins your furniture and clothes, this is the policy to call.

Read your policy for water damage exclusions, especially flood. Ask about limits for electronics and jewelry, and whether you have replacement cost or actual cash value.

If you do not have renters insurance, consider getting it. It is usually inexpensive compared to the value of your belongings.

Keeping records and communication clean

Save every message with your landlord or manager. Write dates and times of calls. A clear record helps if there is a dispute about timing or responsibility.

Be polite and specific. State what happened, what you saw, what you did and what you are asking for. Short written messages work better than long ones.

If the problem is not fixed, a local tenant rights group or an attorney can advise on options. Keep the focus on getting the home dried and safe.

Temporary housing and rent questions

If a unit becomes unusable, the lease and the facts decide what happens. Some leases and policies address temporary housing, and some do not. Ask both the landlord and your renters insurer.

Do not stop paying rent without advice. California has specific rules, and a mistake can lead to an eviction notice. A tenant rights group or an attorney can explain your options for your case.

Keep receipts for hotel stays, meals and storage. Some renters policies reimburse additional living expenses, and the receipts support the request.

Preventing leaks in shared buildings

Tenants can reduce risk by reporting small drips quickly and by keeping drains clear of grease and wipes. Do not ignore a stain on a ceiling or a damp patch on a wall.

Landlords can schedule regular checks of supply lines, water heaters and roofs. A modest inspection routine finds problems before they become emergencies.

What this means in Morgan Hill

Morgan Hill has multi-family housing and rental homes across town, from older downtown buildings to newer complexes. Water often moves between units, so tell the manager if the stain appears on a shared ceiling. In older buildings, supply lines and roofs are common culprits.

Renters in nearby places such as South San Jose or San Martin follow the same state rules, but permits and local services differ. For a unit leak that crosses walls, see water extraction and ceiling water damage repair.
