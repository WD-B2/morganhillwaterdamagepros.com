Water-Damaged Electronics in Morgan Hill, CA: When to Unplug

Wet electronics are both a safety risk and a data risk. Here is the order of operations.

Call (844) 950-6900

By the Morgan Hill Water Damage Pros team | Published November 12, 2026.

Unplug wet electronics only if you can do so without standing in water and without touching a wet outlet. If water reaches outlets or the panel, stay away and call your electric utility or 911 first. Once an item is safely disconnected, do not power it on, remove batteries if you can, and let it dry in a cool place before a technician checks it.

The danger is twofold. Electricity and water together can shock you, and powering on a wet device can short its circuits. A calm, ordered approach protects both you and the data.

Safety first: power and water

If the floor is wet and outlets are near the water, do not enter. Turn off the circuit at the panel only if you can reach it dry. If the panel is wet, call the electric utility.

Never use a household vacuum or extension cord in a wet room. This follows standard electrical safety guidelines. Treat every cord on a wet floor as live until the circuit is off.

What to do with each device

Different gear needs different handling.

Device types and first steps.
DeviceFirst stepAvoid
Laptop or phonePower off, remove battery or case, dry the outsideCharging or turning it on.
TV or monitorUnplug if safe, let dry for several daysPowering on before it dries.

Do not trust rice

Rice is a popular myth. It does not draw water out of a device well, and dust from it can get inside. Silica gel packs and open-air drying work better, and a technician with proper tools works best.

For phones and laptops, ask a repair shop about opening the device and cleaning the board. Corrosion starts quickly and shows up later, so do not assume a device is fine just because it works now.

Protect your data

Data recovery works best when the drive has not been powered up while wet.

  • Back up anything on a cloud service before replacing devices.
  • Hand a wet drive to a data recovery service rather than running it.
  • Write down passwords and account details that lived on the lost device.
  • Contact your carrier or manufacturer about warranty and replacement options.

Insurance and replacement

Electronics damaged by a covered sudden loss may be included in a contents claim. Keep serial numbers, photos of the items and receipts when you have them. The adjuster may ask for age and replacement cost.

Surge protectors and power strips that got wet are normally replaced. See our appliance leak water damage page for appliances and our structural drying page for how a room is dried so devices do not re-wet.

Batteries, chargers and fire risk

Lithium batteries can short and overheat if they get wet and are then charged. Do not charge a wet phone, laptop or power bank. Remove removable batteries and place them on a non-flammable surface.

If a device smells of burning, swells or gets hot, move it outdoors on a hard surface away from anything flammable, and do not handle it with bare hands. Call the fire department if it smokes.

Replace wet chargers and cords. Corrosion inside a plug can cause shorts later even after the cord dries.

Large electronics, HVAC and panels

TVs, stereos and desktop computers can hold charge in capacitors even when unplugged, so do not open them yourself. A technician can open, clean and test them.

Furnaces, water heaters and washing machines have controls that suffer when wet. A technician should check them before restart. Gas appliances need a professional inspection after flooding.

An electrical panel that got wet needs an electrician before it is energized. Do not flip breakers on a panel that has been under water or soaked.

Getting a professional opinion

A repair shop can clean corrosion from circuit boards using solvents and ultrasonic baths. The sooner the device arrives, the better the odds. Ask what they charge for evaluation before work starts.

Compare the cost to replacement. For older devices, replacement may cost less. For devices that hold irreplaceable data, recovery may be worth the expense.

Ask if the shop offers a warranty on repair. Corrosion can return, and a written warranty shows how confident the shop is.

Preventing a repeat

Keep electronics off the floor in rooms prone to leaks, such as laundry rooms, basements and garages. Use shelves or wall mounts.

A leak sensor near the water heater or washer gives early warning. Surge protectors help with power surges, but they do not protect against water.

Back up your data regularly. A cloud backup turns a lost device into an inconvenience instead of a disaster.

What this means in Morgan Hill

Garage workshops and home offices near doors take on water first during a storm. On January 8, 2017, streets such as Llagas Road and Hale Avenue flooded, which is a reminder to keep electronics off the floor in garages and ground-level rooms.

Homes near Coyote and in the Madrone area should raise surge protectors and chargers well above the floor. If your electric panel is in the garage and water rises, call the utility before entering.

Homes in San Martin often run a well pump and panel that also need a dry check. Have an electrician inspect that circuit before power is restored.
